02 — Concept & Problem

Most tools tell you where to go. This one tells you how a place will feel.

The Core Question

Soft City is a map-based system that transforms subjective environmental experiences — noise, crowding, atmosphere — into a clear, actionable metric.

The goal wasn't just to visualize places. The harder question was: how can something as personal and situational as "calmness" become measurable and useful in real time?

The Gap

Location tools optimize for popularity, proximity, and ratings — none account for how a place actually feels.

Calm is subjective, context-dependent, and difficult to quantify. This creates a persistent gap between what a place is (a café, a station) and how it feels in the moment.

What Makes Calm Hard

Calmness is subjective, context-dependent, and often absent from structured location data. It varies by person, time of day, and recent experience — yet it's the first thing people consider when choosing where to spend time.

What Existing Tools Miss

Ratings tell you if people liked a place. Proximity tells you how far it is. Neither tells you whether you'll feel overwhelmed, overstimulated, or at ease when you arrive. The sensory layer is missing entirely.

03 — System Design

Three imperfect signals. One reliable output.

Instead of defining calmness as a fixed value, the system treats it as a dynamic signal. The approach was to break calmness into measurable components, normalize them to a shared scale, and blend multiple imperfect inputs into a single usable score.

01

Human Perception — User Ratings

Users rate sensory factors: noise, crowd density, movement, smell, and lighting. These are normalized and inverted where necessary to produce a consistent calm signal. The most accurate layer — and the least available at launch.

Most Accurate
02

Environmental Assumption — Place Type Baseline

When no user data exists, the system infers calmness from place type. A library is inherently calm; a transit hub is inherently busy. Acts as a predictive starting point — every location is usable immediately.

Predictive
03

Temporal Context — Time Modifier

Calmness isn't static. The system adjusts scores based on time of day and behavioral patterns — rush hour, lunch, nightlife. The same place feels different across time without needing new data.

04 — Making Subjective Data Usable

The challenge wasn't collecting data — it was making it reliable enough to trust.

The Problem

Relying on user ratings alone: sparse at launch, unreliable for new locations, fails before community exists.

The Solution

Blend three signals and weight them by availability. More data means more weight. Missing data means graceful fallback.

Why it works: No single input is trusted as authoritative. Reliability emerges from their combination, not from the quality of any one source.

The Problem

A tool that produces no score when data is missing is useless on day one — before any community has rated any place.

The Solution

Baseline + time modifier ensures every location always has a score. The tool works immediately, improves over time.

Why it works: Graceful degradation is a design requirement, not an afterthought. Systems should fail gracefully, not fail completely.

The Problem

A score without context can be misleading. A "72" from 3 ratings means something very different than a "72" from 300 ratings.

The Solution

Confidence levels are surfaced in the interface alongside every score. Uncertainty is visible — not hidden behind false precision.

Why it works: Trust comes from transparency, not just accuracy. Users calibrate their expectations when they understand the data behind the number.

05 — Interface

Every screen is designed around uncertainty — and what to do with it.

The interface makes the scoring system tangible. Pins on the map show calm scores at a glance. Place detail breaks down sensory fit and confidence. The rating flow feeds directly back into the system.

07 — Outcome

A vague human experience turned into a practical, real-time tool.

System Operational — In Active Development

Soft City successfully converts subjective environmental perception into a structured scoring system. The hybrid model works at full scale — from zero user data to thousands of ratings — without producing empty states or broken scores.

Every location receives a calm score immediately — no community data required to begin
Scores adapt to real-world conditions via temporal context, without needing new ratings
User input continuously improves system accuracy as the community grows
Confidence levels surface in the interface — uncertainty is explicit, not hidden behind false precision
The system remains lightweight and performant while handling three concurrent data sources
08 — Reflection

This project shifted my focus from building features to designing systems.

On Subjectivity

Subjective experiences can be modeled if broken into smaller signals. "Calm" is too large to measure directly — but noise, crowding, and lighting can each be quantified. Their combination approximates the thing itself.

On Data Quality

Imperfect data becomes powerful when combined intelligently. No single input here is reliable. Together — weighted and normalized — they consistently point in the right direction.

On Failure Modes

Systems should degrade gracefully, not fail when data is missing. The baseline-plus-fallback approach ensures the tool is always useful — even before a single user has rated a single place.

On Trust

Trust comes from transparency, not just accuracy. Surfacing confidence levels lets users understand and calibrate their expectations. The score is only part of the answer.
